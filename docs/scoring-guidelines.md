# QA Scoring Guidelines

These guidelines help Quality Analysts score customer support interactions consistently and fairly.

The scorecard uses a 100-point system across five quality categories.

## General Scoring Principles

When evaluating an interaction:

- Score only what can be observed in the interaction.
- Avoid assumptions about the agent's intent.
- Apply the same standards consistently across agents.
- Use partial scores when a requirement is only partially met.
- Add clear QA notes when points are deducted.
- Consider the support channel when applying criteria.
- Follow your organization's policies when they differ from this template.

---

## 1. Communication & Professionalism — 20 Points

### Professional Greeting and Opening — 4 Points

**Full Score (4):**
The agent uses an appropriate greeting and starts the interaction professionally.

**Partial Score (1–3):**
The greeting is present but incomplete, generic, or slightly inappropriate for the channel.

**Zero (0):**
No appropriate greeting is provided when one is required.

### Clear and Easy-to-Understand Communication — 5 Points

**Full Score (5):**
The response is clear, concise, organized, and easy for the customer to understand.

**Partial Score (1–4):**
The message is understandable but contains unnecessary complexity, repetition, or unclear wording.

**Zero (0):**
Communication is confusing or significantly prevents the customer from understanding the resolution.

### Appropriate Tone and Professionalism — 5 Points

**Full Score (5):**
The agent maintains a respectful, helpful, and professional tone.

**Partial Score (1–4):**
The tone is generally acceptable but occasionally sounds robotic, dismissive, or poorly matched to the situation.

**Zero (0):**
The agent uses clearly disrespectful, hostile, or inappropriate language.

### Proper Grammar and Language — 3 Points

**Full Score (3):**
Language is professional and contains no meaningful grammar or spelling problems.

**Partial Score (1–2):**
Minor errors are present but do not affect understanding.

**Zero (0):**
Frequent or serious language errors significantly affect readability or understanding.

### Professional Closing — 3 Points

**Full Score (3):**
The agent appropriately closes the interaction and offers further assistance when applicable.

**Partial Score (1–2):**
A closing is provided but is incomplete or abrupt.

**Zero (0):**
The interaction ends without an appropriate closing when one is expected.

---

## 2. Understanding the Customer's Issue — 20 Points

### Identified the Primary Concern — 7 Points

**Full Score (7):**
The agent correctly identifies the customer's main issue and addresses it.

**Partial Score (1–6):**
The agent understands part of the issue but misses an important detail.

**Zero (0):**
The agent misunderstands or fails to identify the primary concern.

### Relevant Probing Questions — 5 Points

**Full Score (5):**
The agent asks all necessary questions required to understand or resolve the issue.

**Partial Score (1–4):**
Some useful questions are asked, but important information is not collected efficiently.

**Zero (0):**
Required probing is not performed.

**Not Applicable:**
If no probing is reasonably required, teams may award full points or apply their own N/A normalization policy.

### Active Listening / Understanding — 5 Points

**Full Score (5):**
The agent demonstrates that they understood the information already provided by the customer.

**Partial Score (1–4):**
Understanding is generally demonstrated, but one or more details are overlooked.

**Zero (0):**
The agent repeatedly ignores or misunderstands information already provided.

### Avoided Unnecessary Repetition — 3 Points

**Full Score (3):**
The customer is not unnecessarily asked to repeat information.

**Partial Score (1–2):**
Minor unnecessary repetition occurs.

**Zero (0):**
The agent repeatedly requests information that was already clearly provided.

---

## 3. Resolution & Accuracy — 30 Points

### Accurate Information — 10 Points

**Full Score (10):**
All material information provided to the customer is accurate.

**Partial Score (1–9):**
The response contains a minor inaccuracy that does not materially change the resolution.

**Zero (0):**
Materially incorrect information is provided.

### Correct Resolution or Next Steps — 10 Points

**Full Score (10):**
The correct resolution is provided, or the correct next steps are clearly explained.

**Partial Score (1–9):**
The resolution is mostly correct but incomplete.

**Zero (0):**
The resolution is incorrect or fails to address the issue.

### Effective Use of Available Resources — 5 Points

**Full Score (5):**
The agent appropriately uses available tools, knowledge resources, or escalation paths.

**Partial Score (1–4):**
The correct resources are eventually used, but inefficiently or incompletely.

**Zero (0):**
Relevant available resources are ignored, resulting in an incorrect or incomplete outcome.

### Correct Expectations — 5 Points

**Full Score (5):**
Timelines, next steps, limitations, and expectations are communicated accurately.

**Partial Score (1–4):**
Expectations are mostly correct but incomplete or unclear.

**Zero (0):**
Incorrect expectations are set.

---

## 4. Process Compliance — 15 Points

### Required Procedures — 5 Points

Award full points when all applicable required procedures are followed.

Deduct points according to the severity and number of missed procedural steps.

### Required Verification — 4 Points

**Full Score (4):**
All required verification steps are completed correctly.

**Zero (0):**
Mandatory verification is skipped or completed incorrectly.

A serious verification failure may also qualify as a critical error.

### Documentation / Notes — 3 Points

**Full Score (3):**
Required notes are complete, accurate, and useful.

**Partial Score (1–2):**
Notes are present but incomplete.

**Zero (0):**
Required documentation is missing or materially inaccurate.

### Escalation Procedure — 3 Points

**Full Score (3):**
The correct escalation process is followed when escalation is necessary.

**Partial Score (1–2):**
Escalation is appropriate but contains a minor procedural issue.

**Zero (0):**
A required escalation is missed or an incorrect escalation path is used.

---

## 5. Customer Experience — 15 Points

### Empathy — 5 Points

**Full Score (5):**
The agent demonstrates appropriate and genuine acknowledgment of the customer's situation.

**Partial Score (1–4):**
Some acknowledgment is provided but could be more relevant or natural.

**Zero (0):**
Empathy is clearly required but absent, or the response is dismissive.

### Ownership — 5 Points

**Full Score (5):**
The agent takes appropriate responsibility for helping move the issue toward resolution.

**Partial Score (1–4):**
The agent provides assistance but ownership is incomplete.

**Zero (0):**
The customer is unnecessarily redirected or the agent avoids responsibility.

### Customer Effort — 3 Points

**Full Score (3):**
The interaction avoids unnecessary steps, repetition, or transfers.

**Partial Score (1–2):**
Some avoidable effort is created.

**Zero (0):**
The agent creates substantial unnecessary customer effort.

### Positive Closing Experience — 2 Points

**Full Score (2):**
The interaction concludes clearly and appropriately.

**Partial Score (1):**
The closing is acceptable but could be improved.

**Zero (0):**
The interaction ends abruptly or without necessary final information.

---

# Overall Rating

| Final Score | Rating |
| --- | --- |
| 90–100 | Excellent |
| 80–89 | Meets Expectations |
| 70–79 | Needs Improvement |
| Below 70 | Unsatisfactory |

Organizations should customize rating thresholds according to their own quality standards.

---

# Critical Errors

A critical error is a serious failure that may override the numerical QA score.

Possible examples include:

- Serious privacy or data-security violations
- Skipping mandatory customer verification
- Unauthorized account actions
- Material misinformation that could significantly harm the customer
- Serious regulatory or compliance violations
- Intentional interaction avoidance or manipulation

Critical-error definitions should always be aligned with the organization's own policies and applicable requirements.

---

# QA Feedback Standards

When deducting points, QA feedback should explain:

1. What happened
2. Which criterion was affected
3. What the expected behavior was
4. How the agent can improve

Feedback should be specific, evidence-based, and actionable.

## Example

Instead of:

> Agent needs to communicate better.

Prefer:

> The customer asked twice about the expected resolution time, but no timeline was provided. Clearly explain the expected timeline before closing the interaction.

---

# Calibration

QA teams should periodically conduct calibration sessions where multiple evaluators independently score the same interaction and compare results.

Calibration can help:

- Improve scoring consistency
- Identify unclear criteria
- Reduce evaluator bias
- Align expectations across QA analysts and team leaders
- Improve the scorecard over time
